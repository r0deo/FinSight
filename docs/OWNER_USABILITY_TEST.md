# Moderated Rebaz test: 3–5 restaurant/café owners

Status: prepared, not conducted. No participant results or statistical claims are available.

Allow 25–35 minutes per owner. Include a mix of desktop/mobile users and English, Arabic,
and Badini speakers. Obtain permission before observing or recording. Use an isolated test
business; let owners use a realistic example without collecting confidential invoices,
passwords or customer data. Record an anonymous participant code, not their business identity.

## Introduction (2 minutes)

“We are testing the website, not you. Please say what you expect and what you think each result
means. You can stop at any time. These scenarios are assumptions, not forecasts.”

Do not define contribution, lead participants to a preferred response, or help immediately.

## Task 1: Understand a possible cost increase (7 minutes)

Ask: “Enter one product you know. Imagine the supplier raises its unit cost. Find out what
could change if you keep selling at today's price.”

Observe whether they find Simulations → What if conditions change, choose supplier concern,
pick a consistent period and quantity, and read the impact.

Ask:
- “What does the amount remaining mean to you?”
- “Does this number include all your business costs? Is it net profit?”
- “Where did the cost-increase assumption come from? Is the example button a prediction?”

## Task 2: Compare a response and save it (7 minutes)

Ask: “Try one response you might realistically consider, compare it with doing nothing,
and save the scenario so you can find it later.”

Ask:
- “What do you assume about units sold at the new price or with the new supplier?”
- “How much of the decrease is recovered? Is the original position fully recovered?”
- “What do the minimum units mean? Do they guarantee those sales or business break-even?”
- “What else would you need to know about demand, quality and supplier reliability?”

Do not require them to choose the highest numeric contribution.

## Task 3: Record monthly costs without duplication (7 minutes)

Ask: “Enter one actual and one estimated operating cost for a specific month, plus one
inventory purchase. Mark whether each is already included in your product unit costs.
If you do not know, choose the option that fits.”

Then ask them to edit one entry and explain:
- actual versus estimated amounts;
- why buying ingredients is not necessarily consuming them that month;
- whether wages/delivery/electricity may already be in a unit cost;
- what Unsure and an empty month mean;
- why the business's entire rent is not subtracted from one product's contribution.

## Assistance protocol and observations

Allow an initial unaided attempt. If stuck: (1) repeat the goal without pointing;
(2) give a navigation hint; (3) demonstrate only if needed. Log the strongest assistance used.

At the end ask: “Would this help with a real decision this week? Which one? What would stop
you trusting or using it?” Record “no” as readily as “yes”. Capture whether they distinguish
a conditional calculation from a forecast and product contribution from net profit.

## Review after sessions

Summarize observed counts out of the small sample, common misunderstandings and concrete
UI/wording changes. Have a native Badini speaker review financial language independently.
Re-test fixes with owners if possible. Do not call this an A/B test, infer statistical
significance, invent feedback, or claim the small group represents all customers.
